Great interface design is invisible. When software works well, you don't notice the design — you just accomplish what you set out to do. But achieving that invisibility takes extraordinary care.

## Attention to detail

The best interfaces share a quality: every element feels intentional. Spacing isn't arbitrary. Colors aren't random. Transitions aren't decorative. Each decision serves the user's task.

Consider the difference between a 4px and an 8px margin. In isolation, it seems trivial. In context — repeated across hundreds of elements — it's the difference between a layout that feels cramped and one that breathes.

## The role of constraints

Constraints are a designer's best friend. A limited color palette forces clarity. A strict type scale creates rhythm. A narrow viewport demands prioritization.

The paradox of design is that more freedom often produces worse results. When anything is possible, nothing feels cohesive. The best work happens within boundaries.

## Learning from print

Digital designers have much to learn from centuries of typographic tradition. The principles of hierarchy, contrast, alignment, and proximity didn't originate with screens — they were refined over generations of bookmaking, poster design, and editorial layout.
