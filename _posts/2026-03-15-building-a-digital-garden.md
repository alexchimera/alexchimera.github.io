The metaphor of a digital garden has been gaining traction in recent years, and for good reason. Unlike a traditional blog — where posts are presented in reverse chronological order and expected to be finished — a garden is a space for tending ideas over time.

## Why a garden?

Blogs carry an implicit expectation: each post should be complete, polished, and timely. That pressure can be paralyzing. A garden, by contrast, invites you to plant seeds — rough ideas, half-formed thoughts — and return to them as they grow.

## The structure

This site uses a simple system borrowed from the gardening community:

- **Seedlings** are raw, unpolished ideas
- **Budding** notes have been refined but still evolve
- **Evergreen** pieces are mature and well-considered

This isn't about lowering standards. It's about making the creative process visible and giving ideas room to breathe.

## Tools and approach

This garden is built with Jekyll and hosted on GitHub Pages. The simplicity is intentional — I want the focus to be on writing, not infrastructure. Every page is just a Markdown file, version-controlled and easy to update.
